24.0 Early humic acid-induced eustress triggers biostimulant responses in Arabidopsis thaliana: RBOHC-derived H2O2 drives growth promotion while RBOHD enables nutritional reprogramming.

matched: ROS, H2O2, Rboh*, NADPH oxidase*, signaling, auxin*, ABA, cytokinin*, jasmon*, nutrient*, plasma membrane, root
abstract

Root-applied humic acids (HAs) promote plant development, yet the early signaling mechanisms underlying their biostimulant action remain incompletely understood. Here, we show that treatment of Arabidopsis thaliana roots with a sedimentary humic acid (SHA) triggers a transient eustress-like response, characterized by early inhibition of shoot and root growth and increased H2O2 accumulation in root hairs and apical roots. This initial oxidative phase was followed by enhanced shoot and root growth, supporting the view that SHA acts as an adaptive plant modulator through a controlled stress-related signal. Pharmacological H2O2 scavenging and genetic analysis of NADPH oxidase mutants revealed that SHA-induced growth promotion depends on RBOHC/RHD2-mediated H2O2 production in roots. In rhd2 plants, SHA failed to induce the characteristic H2O2 increase and did not promote shoot or root growth. By contrast, rbohd plants retained SHA-induced growth stimulation but showed impaired activation of nutrient transporter and plasma membrane H+-ATPase genes, limited improvement in shoot nutrient use efficiency, and partial root architectural remodeling. These results indicate that RBOHC-derived H2O2 is required for the growth-promoting response, whereas RBOHD cooperates with RBOHC to engage broader nutritional and developmental programs. SHA also induced extensive hormonal reprogramming in roots and shoots; however, disruption of individual ABA, auxin, jasmonate, or cytokinin signaling components did not abolish growth promotion, suggesting partially redundant hormonal regulation downstream of ROS. Overall, our findings identify an RBOHC-H2O2 module as a primary entry point for SHA-mediated biostimulant action and support a model in which root-localized oxidative eustress coordinates hormonal, developmental, and nutritional reprogramming in Arabidopsis.

21.5 A calcium-fingerprint atlas infers the signaling modes of complex elicitors and reveals a salt-like response to a humic biostimulant
Zupunski, M., Brugman … G.
preprintKinases and signal transductionPlant-microbe interactions and immunityGrowthbioRxiv · 2026-10-14
matched: calcium, Ca2+, cytosolic calcium, salt sens*, signaling, receptor*, MAMP*, DAMP*, elicitor*, root, roots, shoot*
abstract

A central challenge in plant signaling is to infer, from an observed response, the mechanism that produced it, including perception and signaling components involved. Cytosolic calcium (Ca2+) is well suited to this reverse-engineering problem: it is a universal early messenger whose spatiotemporal pattern, the Ca2+ signature, is shaped by the specific channels and receptors a stimulus activates. We hypothesize that multiparametric analysis of Ca2+ signals can identify the main signaling pathways activated by complex or poorly defined elicitors such as biostimulants. Here, we establish a quantitative framework to resolve Ca2+ dynamics in roots of Arabidopsis seedlings by transforming spatiotemporal Ca2+ transients into multidimensional signatures. Using a normalization and feature extraction approach (Crestline), we define descriptors capturing the onset time, location along the root axis, duration, and propagation of Ca2+ signals tip- or shootwards, enabling robust comparison across stimuli. Integration of these descriptors reveals stimulus-specific "calcium fingerprints" that allow systematic classification of responses to biotic and abiotic cues within a structured signaling space. To test whether projecting an uncharacterized input into this space can predict its signaling mode, we analyzed a complex humic biostimulant. Its response fell within the fast-onset region of signaling space shared by DAMP and salt responses, clearly apart from MAMP responses, and was altered in moca1, a mutant impaired in salt sensing. Together, these findings establish Ca2+ dynamics as an interpretable encoding layer for environmental information and provide a scalable framework to resolve complex biological inputs.

abstract

Calcium-dependent protein kinases (CDPKs/CPKs) play crucial roles in plant responses to drought stress. In this study, we reveal that ZmCPK1, an atypical CDPK whose kinase activity is calcium-independent, acts as a positive regulator of drought resistance in maize. ZmCPK1 is a constitutively active kinase interacting with and phosphorylates multiple components of the abscisic acid (ABA) signaling pathway, including ZmSLAC1, ZmSnRK2.10, and ZmPP2C11. abstract

Plants integrate multiple environmental cues to decide the timing of seed germination. Light promotes germination primarily through photoreceptor phytochrome B (phyB), which eliminates the action of transcription factor PHYTOCHROME-INTERACTING FACTOR 1 (PIF1).
