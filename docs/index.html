abstract

ABSTRACT Drought is a multifaceted environmental constraint that alters water dynamics across the soil‐plant‐atmosphere system. In order to survive under drought stress, plants must respond at several levels from cellular signalling to whole‐plant hydraulic regulation. Although receptor‐like kinases (RLKs) have been reported to play important roles in these responses, they are often studied within individual molecular pathways rather than as integrators of environmental and physiological processes. Here, we review RLK‐mediated drought signalling organised across three interconnected spatial scales. At the root–soil interface, RLKs perceive osmotic and mechanical cues to regulate root architecture, aquaporin dynamics and apoplastic barrier formation, thereby tuning root hydraulic conductivity. Over long distances, RLK‐peptide modules and reactive oxygen species waves transmit water‐deficit signals from roots to shoots via xylem transport and cell‐to‐cell propagation, while RLK‐dependent vascular differentiation preserves hydraulic safety. In leaves, RLKs modulate stomatal aperture through ion‐channel regulation, ROS‐calcium signalling, cell‐wall mechanosensing and developmental reprogramming of stomatal density. Importantly, we highlight how these pathways operate across distinct temporal scales—from rapid electrochemical responses to long‐term anatomical remodelling—and identify key unresolved questions, including how signalling specificity is achieved among redundant RLK members and how hydraulic and biochemical cues are integrated. By linking RLK‐mediated signalling to whole‐plant physiological responses, this review offers potential avenues for enhancing crop drought resilience through targeted modulation of RLK pathways.

abstract

Soil salinity inhibits plant growth, in part, by restricting the availability of essential mineral nutrients such as iron (Fe). Although Fe deficiency frequently coincides with salt stress in the field, the molecular mechanisms integrating Fe homeostasis with salt tolerance remain largely unknown. Here, we identify the Fe deficiency response regulator bHLH121 as a key coordinator of salt tolerance in Arabidopsis. Loss-of-function mutants of bHLH121 exhibit pronounced hypersensitivity to salt stress, with severely impaired primary root elongation and reduced seedling biomass. abstract

Calcium signals are fundamental for plants and play crucial roles in developmental processes and in rapid responses to environmental stimuli and stress factors. Nevertheless, the mechanisms underlying the decoding of calcium signals in plants are still largely unclear. We have addressed the question of Ca2+ signal decoding in a bottom-up modelling approach starting with the thermodynamics of Ca2+ binding to EF hands (EF: Glutamic acid-Phenylalanine) in Ca2+-binding proteins (CBPs).
